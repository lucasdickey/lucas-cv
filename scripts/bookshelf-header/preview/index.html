Bookshelf header studies

Each header starts from the 3D bookshelf on /real-books. I pointed a straight-on camera at each bookcase and rendered it in separate layers, keeping the real lighting and shadows. Then I drew every layer as a line engraving on the site's cream paper. Following David East's multiplane occlusion post, things move in a middle layer and pass behind the layer in front, so a still picture feels alive.

The four layers, back to front

A

Night Shift

All six bookcases as a skyline. Over 24 seconds a patch of window light drifts across the wall and slips behind each case. At dusk the paper turns navy and the reading lamps come on, cubby by cubby, behind the books.

terminal — bash
Motion is off on this device, so this shows the daytime still.
Moves
Window light on the back wall, hidden behind each case. Lamps sit between the back wall and the books, so books show as silhouettes and the frame crops every glow.
Weight
About 1.0 MB: one daytime image and three night layers. The lamps and light patches are drawn by the browser.
Phones
Crops to the middle of the skyline, centered on the wooden bookcase. Reduced-motion settings get the daytime still.
B

Shelf Express

A close crop of the wooden bookcase's top shelf. A small red engine and its cars run the length of the shelf, ducking behind each of the seven dividers the way the tweet's train runs behind the bridge piers. The books stay black and white, so the train is the only red.

terminal — bash
Motion is off on this device, so the train is parked mid-shelf.
Moves
The train rides on the shelf in front of the books and behind the frame, so each divider hides it for a moment. Wheels turn, smoke puffs drift back, and the fern sways slightly.
Weight
About 0.55 MB for four image layers. The train is a small drawing in the page itself.
Phones
Shows the middle five cubbies of the shelf. Reduced-motion settings get the train parked mid-shelf.
C

Exploded View

The skyline at rest, with red spines picked out like a second printing ink. Move the pointer across it and the layers shift by different amounts, giving a quiet sense of depth. Press the button and the layers swing apart into a stack of cards, like the middle of the tweet's video, then fall back into place.

terminal — bash
Moves
Nothing until you interact. The pointer shifts the back wall, books, frame and top decor by different amounts. The button fans the four layers apart in 3D.
Weight
About 0.9 MB for five layers.
Phones
No pointer, so it sits still until tapped. Tapping the button still separates the layers.
